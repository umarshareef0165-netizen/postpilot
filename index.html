Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 13, 2026

PostPilot is a small, browser-only tool that drafts a day of social posts from a brief you write and schedules them through your Buffer account. This page explains, in plain language, exactly what PostPilot does with your information.

What PostPilot accesses

When you choose to connect Buffer, PostPilot asks Buffer for permission to see your Buffer account and its channels (your connected social profiles) and to create and schedule posts. PostPilot uses that access only to:

  • List your Buffer channels so you can choose where each post goes.
  • Schedule the exact posts you drafted, edited and approved in PostPilot, at the times you picked.

PostPilot never reads your private messages, never posts anything you did not draft and schedule yourself, and never changes your Buffer account settings.

Where your data lives

PostPilot has no server. There is no PostPilot database, no PostPilot account system, and no backend collecting anything.

  • Your brief and your draft posts exist only in the page open in your browser.
  • Your Buffer access token (and refresh token, if Buffer issues one) is stored only in your own browser's localStorage, on your device, so you stay connected between visits. It is never sent anywhere except to Buffer's own API, in the Authorization header of requests you initiate.
  • When you schedule posts, the post text, channel and time are sent directly from your browser to Buffer's API (api.buffer.com) — that is the scheduling you asked for. Nothing is sent to any PostPilot server, because there is none.

What is never done with your data

  • Nothing is sold, rented or shared with third parties.
  • There is no advertising, no analytics and no tracking of any kind in PostPilot.
  • Your brief, drafts and tokens are never used to train models or for any purpose other than scheduling your posts.

Disconnecting and deleting

You can disconnect at any time with the Disconnect button in PostPilot, which removes the stored tokens from your browser's localStorage immediately. You can also revoke PostPilot's access from your Buffer account settings, and clearing your browser's site data removes everything PostPilot stored. Drafts and the on-screen queue disappear when you close or reload the page.

Questions

PostPilot is an independent project built on Buffer's public API. If something in this policy is unclear, disconnect and remove the stored tokens first — that takes effect instantly — and check Buffer's own privacy policy for how Buffer handles your account and scheduled posts.
